## Findings

**MEDIUM — cleanup pidfd is acquired after a PID-file race.** `tests/test_containment_probe.py:35-53` obtains a numeric detached-grandchild PID from a file and later opens a pidfd. If the original process exits and its PID is reused before `pidfd_open`, the handle may refer to an unrelated process; cleanup sends SIGKILL through the handle. A pidfd is stable only after acquisition. Acquire the pidfd while the child remains an unreaped direct child of its parent, and transfer the fd to the test controller, e.g. over a Unix socket using SCM_RIGHTS. Acceptance: simulate child death and PID publication delays, prove cleanup never signals a process whose identity was not pinned at creation.

**MEDIUM — signal 0 does not demonstrate a running detached process.** The same test at line 49 treats `pidfd_send_signal(pidfd, 0)` success as survival. On this host an owned child was observed in `/proc/<pid>/stat` state `Z` and pidfd signal 0 still returned success. Require an explicit post-leader-death bounded liveness handshake by the exact pinned child, rather than relying on existence. Cleanup must verify termination.
